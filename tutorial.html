What You'll Need

Just one thing: a tiny computer called an ESP32.

An ESP32 is a small computer about the size of your thumb. It has WiFi, Bluetooth, and can run programs you write. Professional engineers use these to prototype real products.

You'll also need a USB cable to connect it to your computer — but most ESP32 kits include one.

ESP32 Dev Board ~$12

This is yours to keep forever. You can reprogram it as many times as you want, use it for future projects, or just keep it as a reminder of what you built.

Let's Fix It

Something not working? That's completely normal — even professionals run into these issues. Let's figure it out together.

  • "No device found" or nothing appears

    The most common issue! Try a different USB cable. Most cables only charge and can't transfer data. Look for one that came with a phone or hard drive.

  • The flash starts but fails partway

    Try a different USB port on your computer. Sometimes ports are flaky. Also: unplug the ESP32, wait 5 seconds, and plug it back in.

  • Flash completes but LED doesn't breathe

    Unplug the ESP32 and plug it back in. It needs a fresh start to run the new program. Still nothing? Try flashing again.

  • Mac: Can't find the serial port

    You might need a driver. Search "CH340 driver Mac" and install it. Then restart your browser.

  • Windows: Driver problems

    Get the CP210x driver from Silicon Labs. Install, restart browser, try again.

  • Still stuck?

    Email wja326@lehigh.edu — seriously, I want to help. Include what you tried and what happened.

What Is This?

The Short Version

CRUMB is a free, open-source project that teaches electronics by doing, not watching. Genesis Zero is the first step: prove to yourself that you can program a computer.

Why I Built This

I watched my brother struggle with technology that wasn't designed for how his brain works. I realized the problem isn't people — it's that we've made technology harder to understand than it needs to be.

Every computer — your phone, your car, the chip in your hand — works the same way: Input → Process → Output. Once you see that pattern, the mystery disappears.

The Philosophy

  • Do, then understand. You learn to ride a bike by riding, not by studying physics. Same with programming.
  • No gatekeeping. All the code is public. Every line is explained. Ask any question.
  • Yours forever. The hardware is yours. The knowledge is yours. No subscriptions, no accounts.
